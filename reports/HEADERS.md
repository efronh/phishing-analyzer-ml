# Sender authentication: SPF, DKIM, DMARC

Generated by `experiments_headers.py`. Protocol, written before the first run: [`HEADERS_PROTOCOL.md`](HEADERS_PROTOCOL.md). Measurement only; nothing is added to the model.

- **Phishing:** 1571 Nazario emails from 2019–2024 (training corpus). Phishing Pot and the lockbox are kept back for a later blind test.
- **Legitimate:** 676 of the author's promotional emails, **without** the DMARC part of the junk filter (46 sender domains, at most 30 each). They are private; this report holds rates only.

## Verified sender (exploratory, added after the result)

Not in the protocol. A **verified sender** passes DMARC and carries a DKIM signature aligned with its `From` domain: the mail really comes from the domain it shows. That does not make the domain honest; phishers can authenticate their own lookalike domains.

| group | verified sender |
|---|---:|
| Nazario 2019-24 (phishing) | 27.0% (24.9–29.2) |
| promotions (legitimate) | 95.0% (93.0–96.4) |

## Caveats

- **No mailing-list baseline:** the archives strip these headers, so the only legitimate comparison is one inbox of promotions.
- **`from_freemail` looks stronger than it is:** real legitimate mail includes person-to-person mail from freemail addresses, which a promotions-only set lacks.
- **Forwarding:** many Nazario messages carry ARC headers, a sign of forwarding, which can break SPF and DMARC. The computed checks (`dkim_not_aligned`, `return_path_not_aligned`, `reply_to_other_domain`) do not depend on the receiver.
